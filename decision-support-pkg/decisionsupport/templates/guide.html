{# ================================================================ #} {# WHEN TO USE #} {# ================================================================ #}
When to use which framework

Each framework is suited to a different type of problem. Use this table to pick the right tool before you start.

Framework Use when… Not ideal when… Time required
Cynefin You are unsure what type of decision you are facing — should you apply a known procedure, consult experts, experiment, or just act? Good first step before choosing any other framework. You already know the domain and just need a process for deciding. 15–30 min for assessment
Delphi You need structured consensus from multiple experts or stakeholders — especially when participants might be influenced by seniority or peer pressure in a room. Good for forecasting, prioritisation, and policy decisions. Speed is critical, or you have fewer than 3–4 participants. One person’s opinion is sufficient. Hours to days across rounds
Pre-Mortem You are about to commit to a plan and want to stress-test it. Especially valuable for projects with a high cost of failure, or where optimism bias and groupthink may be present. The decision is reversible and low-stakes. The plan is not yet concrete enough to imagine its failure. 30–60 min brainstorm session
Matrix You have two or more concrete options and a clear set of criteria that matter. Good for vendor selection, technology choices, location decisions, or any structured trade-off analysis. Options are not yet defined, or criteria are unknown. The decision is primarily emotional or political. 30–90 min for scoring
Six Hats You want rich, multi-perspective thinking from a group without debate or domination. Good for creative problem-solving, strategy sessions, and any topic where you want to systematically explore all angles. You need a specific quantitative answer. The group is very small (1–2 people). 60–90 min for all six hats
{# Quick decision guide #}
Quick decision guide

“I don’t know what kind of problem this is.”

→ Start with Cynefin to classify the domain, then choose your next framework accordingly.

“I need to choose between Option A, B, and C.”

→ Use Decision Matrix to score them against your criteria objectively.

“I need experts to agree on priorities.”

→ Use Delphi for anonymous, iterative consensus building.

“We’re about to launch — what could go wrong?”

→ Use Pre-Mortem to surface hidden risks before you commit.

“I want the team to think deeply without debate.”

→ Use Six Hats to explore all perspectives systematically.

“I need to do all of the above.”

→ Cynefin first → Pre-Mortem to stress-test → Delphi or Matrix to decide → Six Hats for buy-in.

{# ================================================================ #} {# PARTICIPANT GUIDE #} {# ================================================================ #}
Participant guide

If someone has sent you a link, this section explains what to expect. You do not need an account. Your responses are anonymous unless you choose to enter your name.

Delphi What participants do

You will see the session question and the current round’s prompt.

  • Early rounds: Write a free-text response explaining your view. Add an optional score (1–9).
  • Rating rounds: Rate each proposition 1–9 (1 = strongly disagree, 9 = strongly agree) and optionally explain your rating.
  • Between rounds: You will see which rounds are closed and their summaries, but not other participants’ scores.

There is no login. You can reload the page and submit again.

Pre-Mortem What participants do

You will see the plan being evaluated. Imagine it is one year later and the project has failed.

  • Describe one specific reason why it failed — be candid and concrete.
  • Your response is fully anonymous.
  • You can submit multiple scenarios by reloading and submitting again.

If the link shows “closed”, brainstorming has ended and the facilitator is reviewing your input.

Matrix What participants do

You will see a grid of options and criteria. Score each option against each criterion.

  • Scores are 1–10 (10 = best possible on that criterion).
  • Criteria weights are hidden from participants to prevent anchoring.
  • Enter your name or leave it blank to stay anonymous.

Partial submissions are fine — score as many cells as you can.

Six Hats What participants do

You will see which hat is currently open and its thinking prompt.

  • Write a contribution from that hat’s perspective — don’t filter yourself.
  • You can contribute as many times as you like while the hat is open.
  • When the facilitator closes a hat and opens the next, you will see the update on reload.

Think of each hat as a costume: your job is to think from that perspective only, not to debate others.

{# ================================================================ #} {# ADMIN GUIDE — OVERVIEW #} {# ================================================================ #}
Admin guide — overview

As the facilitator, you manage the full session lifecycle. The navbar gives you access to all five frameworks. Use the Results page for a unified view across everything.

General controls (all frameworks)
  • Archive — hides a session from the main list; data is fully preserved and visible in Results. Use for completed sessions you don’t need to see daily.
  • Delete — permanently removes all data (with a confirmation dialog). Cannot be undone.
  • Show archived — toggle on any list page to reveal archived sessions. Archived sessions can be restored.
  • Results dashboard — at /results, shows all sessions across all frameworks with a Save as PDF button.
Participant links
  • Every session with a participant view shows a Participant link banner when the session is in the correct state (e.g. brainstorming open, round open, scoring open).
  • The link is public — anyone with it can participate. No login required.
  • Participants never see raw scores, other participants’ names, or admin controls.
{# ================================================================ #} {# CYNEFIN HOW-TO #} {# ================================================================ #}
Cynefin How to run a classification
  1. 1
    Create a decision
    Click “+ New decision”. Give it a clear title and an optional description. The status starts as open.
  2. 2
    Answer the assessment questions
    Work through the 7 diagnostic questions on the decision page. Each covers a different lens: precedent, causality, stakeholder agreement, expertise, reversibility, urgency, and information quality. You can answer all at once or return later.
  3. 3
    Read the domain classification
    After saving, the tool scores your answers and suggests a Cynefin domain. Read the domain guidance card — it explains what the domain means and gives concrete action recommendations.
  4. 4
    Add context
    Use the Notes panel to attach meeting transcripts, key facts, or stakeholder input. Use Actions to record next steps and mark them resolved as you go.
  5. 5
    Update the status
    Move the decision through open → decided → closed as you progress. Archive when done.
Tips
  • Re-answer questions as your understanding improves — responses overwrite previous answers and the domain re-classifies automatically.
  • Upload a transcript via “Attach transcript” to auto-populate notes from VTT or plain-text files.
  • If scores are tied the tool returns “Disorder” — gather more information before deciding.
{# ================================================================ #} {# DELPHI HOW-TO #} {# ================================================================ #}
Delphi How to run a consensus session
  1. 1
    Create a session
    Click “+ New session”. Write a clear, specific question. Set a consensus threshold — IQR ≤ 2.0 is the standard Delphi threshold for a 1–9 scale.
  2. 2
    Open Round 1
    Click “Open Round 1”. Add an optional prompt for participants. Share the participant link.
  3. 3
    Collect free-text responses
    In Round 1, participants write qualitative responses. When everyone has responded, click “Close round”.
  4. 4
    Distil propositions
    Read all responses and add key themes as items (propositions) for rating. Each item should be a clear, rateable statement.
  5. 5
    Open Round 2 for rating
    Open the next round. Participants rate each item 1–9. The tool shows IQR and a consensus verdict per item in real time.
  6. 6
    Repeat until consensus
    Close the round, review where IQR is still high, open another round with a prompt addressing the disagreement. Repeat until all items reach consensus or you decide to stop.
  7. 7
    Archive when done
    When consensus is reached (or you have concluded), archive the session. The Results dashboard shows the final state.
Tips
  • Keep items to 5–10 per round — too many causes fatigue.
  • Include the previous round’s median in the Round 2 prompt to encourage convergence.
  • Anonymity is key — don’t share who said what between rounds.
  • 2–3 rounds is typical. If you haven’t converged in 4, consider restructuring the items.
{# ================================================================ #} {# PRE-MORTEM HOW-TO #} {# ================================================================ #}
Pre-Mortem How to run a risk session
  1. 1
    Create a session
    Click “+ New session”. In the “Plan being evaluated” field, write a clear description of the plan or initiative. The more concrete, the better — participants need to be able to imagine it failing.
  2. 2
    Set status to Brainstorming
    Change the status to “Brainstorming”. The participant link banner will appear. Share the link with your team.
  3. 3
    Let participants submit scenarios
    Participants visit the link and complete the sentence “We failed because…”. Encourage specific, concrete scenarios — not generic risks. Submissions are anonymous.
  4. 4
    Switch to Reviewing
    When enough scenarios have come in (allow 24–48 hours for async), change status to “Reviewing”. The submission form closes for participants.
  5. 5
    Consolidate into risks
    Read each scenario. Group similar ones and add consolidated risks with a title, description, severity (1–5), likelihood (1–5), and mitigation note.
  6. 6
    Present and act
    Use the risk register in your planning meeting. Focus on High severity + High likelihood risks. Update the plan to address the top risks, then mark the session complete.
Tips
  • Frame the brief clearly: “It is one year from now. The project has failed spectacularly. Why?” — specificity produces better scenarios.
  • 10–15 scenarios is a healthy number. More people = richer input.
  • Focus mitigation effort on High × High risks first.
  • Don’t aim to prevent every risk — just the high-impact ones.
{# ================================================================ #} {# MATRIX HOW-TO #} {# ================================================================ #}
Matrix How to run a decision matrix
  1. 1
    Create a matrix
    Click “+ New matrix”. Give it a descriptive title such as “CRM platform selection — Q3”.
  2. 2
    Add options
    Add each alternative being considered. 2–6 options is the sweet spot.
  3. 3
    Add criteria and set weights
    Add the criteria that matter for this decision. Set a weight 1–5 for each (5 = most important). Weights are hidden from participants to prevent anchoring. Agree weights with stakeholders before seeing scores.
  4. 4
    Open scoring
    Change the status to “Scoring”. The participant link appears. Share it with everyone who should score.
  5. 5
    Collect scores
    Participants score each option 1–10 per criterion. Multiple participants can score — their scores are averaged per cell.
  6. 6
    Review results
    Change status to “Closed”. Options are ranked by weighted average. The top option is highlighted. If the winner isn’t the intuitive choice, use it as a conversation starter.
Tips
  • Keep criteria independent — overlapping criteria effectively double-weight a factor.
  • Weight 1 = “nice to have”, weight 5 = “deal-breaker”.
  • 5–8 criteria is optimal. More than 10 causes decision fatigue.
  • The matrix ranks, it doesn’t decide — use it to structure the conversation.
{# ================================================================ #} {# SIX HATS HOW-TO #} {# ================================================================ #}
Six Hats How to run a parallel thinking session
  1. 1
    Create a session
    Click “+ New session”. Write a clear topic or question in the “Topic” field — this is what participants will think about throughout the session.
  2. 2
    Share the participant link
    Copy the participant link from the session page and share it before the meeting. Participants can join from any device.
  3. 3
    Open the White Hat
    Click “Open White Hat”. Participants see the white hat prompt and submit factual observations, data, and what information is missing. There is no debate — only contributions.
  4. 4
    Close and move to the next hat
    When contributions slow down (5–15 minutes per hat in a live meeting), click “Close White Hat”. Immediately open the Red Hat. Repeat through all six.
  5. 5
    Recommended order
    White → Red → Black → Yellow → Green → Blue. Blue last provides synthesis and summary. The session auto-completes after all six are closed.
  6. 6
    Review contributions
    After completion, read the contributions by hat on the admin page. Use the Blue hat contributions as the meeting summary and next-step guide.
Hat reference
  • 🤍 White Facts & data
  • ❤️ Red Emotions & gut
  • 🖤 Black Risks & caution
  • 💛 Yellow Benefits & optimism
  • 💚 Green Ideas & creativity
  • 💙 Blue Process & summary
Tips
  • Enforce the hat: if someone argues during Red hat, redirect them — “Red hat only: feelings, no justification.”
  • 5–10 min per hat is typical in a live session.
  • For async sessions, leave each hat open for 24 hours.
